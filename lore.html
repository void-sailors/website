LORE

in the Hand-Lettered Volume

Many names have been given to magic.

Fire. Light. Curse. Gift.

People prefer simple words. They fit neatly into stories where the world behaves and power chooses sides. Those words were used freely once. They were believed, for a time.

Magic was never a thing to be taken.

It is imagined as fire in a brazier — seized, shaped, and spent until the coals go cold. Power is spoken of as a weapon, or as a blessing handed down by favor or by fate.

This is wrong.

Magic is not fire. Magic is the sea.

A ship does not own the water beneath its hull. It does not command the wind in its sails. It survives by learning the currents, by respecting them, by never forgetting how small it is.

A sailor who curses the sea will drown. A sailor who worships it will drown beside him. Only the one who learns its moods and its limits comes home.

So it was with magic, before the world learned humility the hard way.

❦

Imagine the world as a barrel lowered into an endless ocean.

The wood of that barrel was the realm — solid, familiar, bound by rules men were certain they understood. Inside it, life followed patterns. Seasons turned. People aged. Empires rose, convinced they would never fall.

Outside the barrel was the Deep. Limitless. Pressing. Patient.

For ages uncounted, the barrel held.

But curiosity is a sharp thing, and the first mages did what sailors have always done at the edge of the map.

They bored a hole.

When the wall between realms was pierced, the Deep came through. Not with malice. Not with mercy. With certainty.

It ground against the wood. It turned unseen wheels inside the world. That grinding — that friction between what is and what is not — is what we have since called magic.

Energy born not of creation, but of difference.

Like a mill turned by tidewater, it flowed wherever the breach allowed. And where it flowed, wonders followed. Cities lit themselves without flame. Ships crossed the sky as easily as hulls had once crossed water. Hunger, sickness, distance — these became inconveniences instead of laws.

And the people rejoiced.

Magic had no loyalty and no ambition. A current carries a rescue ship as readily as it carries a raider. What shaped magic was never the power. It was the hand on the rudder.

The greatest lie ever told was this: it will always be this way.

❦

More holes were drilled. More gates were opened — not because they were needed, but because they were useful. Magic was spent on spectacle, on vanity, on conveniences that would have shamed a candle.

Nobody measured the pressure. Nobody asked how much a barrel could hold.

It was not war that broke the world. It was greed.

It did not fall to fire, or frost, or storm.

It burst.

Like a barrel packed with gold until the staves split, the realm gave way under its own abundance. The walls buckled. Reality cracked. Islands tore free and drifted off like wreckage.

When the world broke, the sea was gone.

The tides did not retreat. They were unmade. What borders us now is not the Deep. It is the void — not water, not air, but absence. Cold and endless. It does not flow. It pulls.

The magic that once roared like a river scattered into fragments, into eddies, into dying streams.

What remains is not an ocean.

It is a creek.

A thin, stubborn trickle, fed by portals that cannot be closed, only endured. The age of wonders did not end with a scream. It ended with a long, hollow silence.

❦

Once, the Gatekeepers were masters of the tide. They charted the currents between realms. They knew which breaches could be widened and which must never be touched. Their towers stood as lighthouses through an age of power without restraint.

Now they are old men. Quiet women. Wanderers who speak in riddles that nobody wishes to hear.

Spells are not cast as they once were. Not because they cannot be. Because the cost is remembered.

A Gatekeeper does not ask what can be done.

He asks what must never be done again.

❦

Many believe that evil destroyed the world. This is a comfort. Evil is easy to hate.

The truth is worse.

Every mage believed his own use was justified. Every portal was opened just this once. Every small convenience was borrowed against a future that no one intended to repay.

No tyrant stood at the center of the ruin.

Only countless hands reaching into the tide, each certain that they were different.

❦

Magic is not a gift. It is not a curse. It is a force that remembers every breach ever made.

Every spell matters now. Every choice weighs more than it once did.

Because the creek is drying.

And when it is gone —

there will be no tide left to blame.

❦

I have written this. I am old, and I have not changed it.

Every trial won. The rest of the voyage is in the book.
