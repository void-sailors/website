The Armada Gate

It was the squeaky IKEA chair that betrayed him first.

Dexter Blackwell went still. He hadn’t known he was moving until the chair said so.

The dorm room was loaned to him for the week of the contest. Bed, desk, window, chair. Somebody else’s poster tape on the wall. On the desk the sponsored laptop had spun its fans up before he asked it for anything, and the factory film was still on the edges of the lid.

The controller was his. He’d brought it from home. The thumbsticks were worn smooth in exactly the right places, and he held it the way other people held coffee.

If he lost here there was no scholarship. No scholarship, no college. Nobody in his life had a second plan for him.

He launched the client.

The logo appeared, and VoidSailors filled the screen. The game had been revived just for this event, dragged back from obscurity by a YouTuber with money and a point to prove. Dexter logged in as TenScore.

“This is wicked,” he said, to nobody.

The game called it the waiting room. It wasn’t a room. Jagged rock ran out in every direction, and the horizon bent until there was no telling where the ground stopped and the sky started.

And it was quiet. Designed quiet, the kind that made his own breathing sound loud in the headset and made him sit very still without deciding to.

The portal stood in front of TenScore. Two stone arms formed the columns, wrists interlocked, the right hand clasped inside the left, and between them a crystal pulsed slow and even. Above the arms someone had cut a single sigil into the stone. ⍼. A shape of crossed hooks. Players who didn’t know what it meant looked at it anyway.

Other contestants came into existence around him. Some stood and stared. Some spammed emotes. A few jumped in place. One sat down, logged out, and didn’t come back.

He counted them before he thought about why. It came apart at the third row. The shapes shifted and took the number with them, and starting over from the left went worse.

In the corner of the screen a counter flicked from 174 to 173. TenScore half-noticed it, the way you notice a light going off in a house across the street.

Then the portal moved.

Colors turned over inside the frame, slow and aurora-bright, folding into each other and coming apart again.

[All 173 players are in.]

“Look at these arms!” somebody shouted.

“Well, duh.”

“Armada! Ha! Sounds like Arm-Duh!”

The chat bubbles were up over the crowd inside of ten seconds. ARMADA. Arm-duh. ARM-DUH GATE LOL. Nothing official about any of it. It would stick anyway.

Then the ground tilted, or the game decided it had, and every avatar on the rock started sliding toward the gate. Not fast. There was no arguing with it either.

He caught himself sitting very still again.

Each column ran up past where he could tilt the camera. TenScore went between them and was swallowed.

The tunnel twisted. Colors ran past in streaks, and the walls pulsed narrow and wide, narrow and wide, on the same count as the crystal. Somewhere in the middle of it he tasted the protein bar again, and then the coffee.

Then it stopped, and there was an island.

Floating fragments of land hung above the horizon and dragged their shadows slowly across the ground. The trees spiralled. Their bark was purple and they leaned further than trees lean. The grass underfoot was orange, streaked through with mint green, and the whole island rose and fell, very slowly.

The wind moved through the trees at the wrong speed. Every time a fragment passed overhead something chimed, faint and far off, like a bell underwater. It didn’t sound composed. It sounded grown.

The ground at the mouth of the portal was not clean. Broken stone, split branches, a piece of timber with a square hole through it.

Players were already bending for it. Two of them got a hand on the same stone at the same time, and neither of them made anything of it.

Notifications sorted the players as they came out of the portal. Capitalists in green. Socialists in red. Pirates in brown.

The color menu gave up on that one.

[Assigned to: Capitalist Faction — GREEN]

He read it twice.

[Truce active. No player may damage another player.]

He read that one once and forgot it inside the minute.

“You are a mogul, Dexter,” he announced.

Nobody had asked. He said the next part louder anyway.

“Boys on the left, girls on the right, and everyone currently questioning their life choices, please form a stylish diagonal.”

Two players laughed. Someone did a dance emote. It lasted about four seconds and he’d take it.

Then he saw the one in black.

The notifications were still flashing green over everybody’s head, and his was green too. Nothing else on him was. Black from hood to boots, not a scrap of faction color anywhere.

Player wearing black in the green faction, TenScore thought. That’s a bit off.

Then the crowd moved and he lost him.

Behind him somebody was running roll call. “Red team here! Move it! Move it!”

“Somebody’s enjoying that,” TenScore said.

Two red players went past mid-argument.

“—no classes, dude. No races either. It’s all gear.”

“That’s so dumb. Then what did I grind for—”

“Nothing. That’s the point. Everybody starts broke.”

Then a voice arrived beside him, low and pitched for a bigger room.

“Welcome to the team, TenScore.”

Dexter came halfway out of the chair. TenScore turned like nothing had happened, and then had to look up.

“H-hi.”

“I’m Axer Justice.” He tipped his head toward the nameplate above him. “But you already read that. It’s right there.”

“This way.” He was already walking.

TenScore went after him. “Do you have a shorter version of your name?”

“AJ.”

“Tenfold.”

“That’s longer than TenScore.”

“It’s cooler, though.”

“It’s longer.”

A notification opened.

[Daily Objectives]

1 Assemble a crew of four players.

2 Choose a name for your faction.

3 Vote on your leader.

4 Establish and secure your territory.

(Tasks may be completed in any order.)

All across the island crews were forming and coming apart. Two players who had known each other for thirty seconds were already arguing about which of them was in charge of the other.

An invite came in from AJ. When TenScore looked up from it, AJ had stopped walking and was waiting on him. He didn’t say anything about it.

“Check your MIB.” He pronounced it MEEB. “Message in the Bottle. Start gathering now. In an hour there’ll be nothing left on this rock worth bending down for.”

“An hour,” TenScore said. “Optimistic.”

Another notification.

[EverLake is requesting to join your crew.]

“Accept her,” AJ said.

She arrived at a run, cloak river-blue and streaming out behind her.

“Hi. Thanks for letting me join.” She didn’t wait to be asked anything. “Don’t worry, I’m great at healing and terrible at decisions. So I’ll be useful and non-threatening.”

“Where’d you learn healing?” TenScore asked.

She held up a small stone between two fingers and turned it until it caught the light.

“That’s not an answer.”

“Picked it up by the gate.” Her voice dropped. “Turns out it does things.”

AJ shook his head. The hammer on his belt was new enough that the haft still had bark down one side. “Mine was less pretty. Made it while you were doing the diagonal. It’s good for hitting things. Repeatedly.”

“See? Teamwork.” EverLake was already moving off toward the tree line. “I keep you alive, you keep them from turning me into glitter.”

They worked through the bright undergrowth for a while, taking what the ground offered.

It came up easily. The low stalks snapped off short, three to a clump and never two, and the broken ends were wet and a brighter green than the rest of the plant. Under the orange grass the soil was pale all the way down, with no dark in it anywhere.

Nothing had rotted here. Nothing had been here long enough to.

End of Chapter One
