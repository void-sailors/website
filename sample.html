Appointment in the Storm

Blue came down the dock counting on his fingers, and he would have walked straight past them.

“We were coming to find you,” TenScore said.

Blue stopped. He finished the count before he looked up, and then he let it go.

“Quartermaster walked. Middle of the build. No word, no warnin’. One minute he’s on the crew panel, next there’s a gap where he used to be.”

“The speech?”

“Fella on the ramp told us we could walk away.” Blue tipped his head back along the berths. “So he walked.”

TenScore followed the look. At the top of the scaffolding on the mismatched hull, a slate hung from a nail by its cord. Chalk tallies ran down it in bundles of five, and the last bundle stopped at three.

He hadn’t finished with it when Moss spoke.

“A speech is a crowbar, Blue. Only works on a board that’s already loose.”

“He weren’t loose this mornin’.”

“Then it were a good speech.” Moss turned Lucky-Smoky over once and pointed the stem at Tim. “Well. If ye’re a hand short, Moss can spare ye Tim here. Cabin boy. Trips over everythin’ on deck before the rest of us know it’s there.”

Tim straightened. “I have never tripped, Mr. Moss. I have records.” He checked them. “You have fallen over your own inventory twice today.”

Moss snapped his fingers at him. “Exactly! Perfect fit!”

Blue looked from Tim to the plate over TenScore’s head.

TenScore — Nomad

“And him?”

“Ten rides along. Passenger.”

“Passenger’s fine.” Blue was talking to TenScore now. “She won’t give ye a station, lad. Ain’t personal. Ships are particular.”

“I know.”

Then Blue turned to Spag.

“If Spag were takin’ the helm, I’d count it an honor to sail under him. Wouldn’t feel right otherwise.”

“That so?”

“Some men steer ships. Some keep ’em afloat. I know which one I am.”

“Who built her?”

“I did.”

Moss pointed the stem at Spag. “Builder’s call. Ye said as much yerself, by the cannon.”

Spag chewed on that quietly. Long enough that Blue almost took it back.

“Alright. I’ll captain. You keep her together.”

Blue tapped two fingers to his bandana. “Then she’ll sail straight.”

“Four hands and a passenger.” Moss swept the pipe over all of them. “Rust and ruin, we’ve stitched ourselves a crew.”

Spag looked once across the dock, to where three players in clean coats had watched him earlier. Nobody was there now. He reached into his inventory and set a small crate on the boards between them, and he didn’t explain it.

Blue looked at it once. Then he picked it up and tucked it under his arm.

TenScore walked wide of it the whole way over.

The ship sat two platforms along in her dry supports, plates mismatched, the scaffold still standing against her port side. Up close it was bare ribs, evenly spaced, and through them TenScore could see the next berth over and the hull resting in it. Blue set the crate down near the ramp and turned it upright. Nobody decided it was a table. It just became one.

“Six modules short,” Blue said. “Five, if I rob one off the stern.”

Moss weighed his pouch in his palm. “Moss didn’t win twenty shards to keep ’em warm.”

He went off down the berths with it in his fist.

Blue tipped a bucket of tools out across the deck and handed them round without asking anybody what they were good with. A mallet went to Tim. A pry bar went to TenScore.

Spag didn’t take one. He had gone up onto the hover-sail’s spar and was working out along it from the mast, testing each fitting in turn and skipping none of them. Near the tip he found a pin seated crooked. He drew it, set it true, and moved on to the next.

Blue watched him do that much. Then he sorted the last of the tools back into the bucket.

“Old sailors paid good money for a black cat. Ship with one aboard come home. Kept the storms off her.”

Tim turned the mallet over twice before he answered. “I have not seen a cat anywhere in Pirate Bay, Mr. Blue.”

“Seen a C.A.T.” Blue pointed the empty bucket at TenScore.

“Former.” TenScore held up the pry bar. “And I’m a passenger.”

“Cats don’t take stations neither. They just come aboard.”

Up on the spar, Spag moved to the next fitting. “And a cat picks its own ship.”

TenScore took the pry bar aft, to the stern module Blue meant to rob.

Moss came back pulling a handcart, the modules stacked on it and the pouch flat against his belt.

Blue stared at the cart. “That’s every shard ye had.”

“Moss is investin’. Moss expects a cabin with a window.”

They worked. Blue on the frame, Tim holding each module square while Blue set it, Moss at the handcart calling out sizes nobody had asked him for. TenScore carried, two planks at a time. He had learned the number on another ship. The gaps between her ribs closed in order, working aft from the bow.

Tim took the slate down off its nail, read the tallies through to the end, and hung it back exactly where it had been.

Spag came down off the spar when the long timbers started going up. Between boards he watched the far berths. Then he went back to work.

00:21:40 remaining.

The last module was seated a little before the lanterns came up across the bay. TenScore walked the length of her port side from the inside. There wasn’t a gap in it anywhere.

00:04:12 remaining.

Blue stood at the slot in the middle of her deck with the Power Crystal cupped in both hands. He didn’t move right away.

“Never get used to this part. Whole dock goes quiet like she’s listenin’.”

“Then why now?” TenScore asked. “The server’s about to close.”

“That’s why. Ship that sleeps without a captain wakes up wonderin’ whose she is.”

Moss leaned toward TenScore. “Superstitious lot, sailors. Always talkin’ to things that can’t talk back.”

“Aye.” Blue didn’t apologize for it. “Rush her and she hums wrong. And she’ll remind ye of it the whole voyage.”

Tim had moved two steps back to watch. “Statistically the insertion window does not—”

“Tim. Not now.”

Tim closed his mouth.

Blue turned and held the crystal out to Spag on both palms, unhurried, the whole of it offered at once.

TenScore watched Blue’s hands and nothing else.

Spag reached for it.

Boots came up the ramp.

Five of them, in a loose arc, their coats clean and buttoned to the collar. Every plate read Independent Sails, the same as Spag’s. Three of them TenScore had last seen across the dock, watching Spag cross to the cannon. Nobody had a weapon out.

One of them stepped forward. “Spag.”

Spag stopped a few inches short of it. “You’re early.”

The pirate raised an open palm. It wasn’t aimed at Spag. It was aimed at what Blue was holding. “Not today.”

Blue dropped the crystal back into his inventory. Had to put his hands somewhere.

“You know why we’re here.”

Spag didn’t pretend otherwise.

Tim had taken half a step forward. “This was planned. But nobody is angry. That means the outcome is already decided.”

“Good read, kid.” Spag sounded almost glad somebody had made it.

Blue’s hand went to his bandana and came away again. “Void-blasted, hull-sunk nonsense! Ye don’t walk onto a man’s berth and lift his captain off the deck!”

“Is he one?” The pirate looked down at the slot. Nothing was seated in it. “Whose ship is she?”

Blue opened his mouth and shut it again.

Spag didn’t answer for him. Tim’s hand had started to come up.

“Moss’s.”

Moss pointed Lucky-Smoky at the new port side. “Every board on that side’s bought and paid for. Twenty shards. Ask any stall on the dock.”

The pirate looked at Moss, and then at Blue. Blue had folded his arms.

“Since when does Moss build ships?”

“Since Moss lost one.”

He let it stand. “Then fly her, Moss.”

Tim’s hand went back down to his strap.

“Ye don’t just take a captain,” Blue said. “Not like this.”

“We’re not taking him. He’s coming.”

Spag turned then and faced the crew.

“It’s alright.”

Moss lowered the pipe. “Son of the Void. Ye see the knives comin’ and ye walk at ’em anyway. Like a man keepin’ an appointment in a storm.”

“Someone has to go first.”

Red letters came up across the middle of TenScore’s screen. He had seen them once before, lying in the leaves.

SERVER TIME LIMIT REACHED — LOGGING OUT IN 10…

He didn’t read the numbers after that. He was watching Spag.

Spag was looking at Blue. “The crate. You know what to do with it.”

Blue unfolded his arms. Then he nodded, once, hard, the way men nod when they’re agreeing to something they don’t fully understand yet but will.

“You coming back?” TenScore asked.

Spag found him over Blue’s shoulder. “Ask me tomorrow.”

In the chair, Dexter had taken his hands off the controller and hadn’t put them anywhere.

The slot in the middle of the deck stood open with nothing in it. Then the screen went black.
